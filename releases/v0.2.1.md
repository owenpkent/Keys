> **Installing on Windows: about the SmartScreen prompt.**
> On first launch you may see a blue "Windows protected your PC" screen. This is normal for newer apps and does **not** mean anything is wrong. Keys is digitally signed by **OK Studio Inc.** Click **More info** and you will see that publisher name, then click **Run anyway** to install. The prompt stops appearing on its own as more people install. (Microsoft removed the SmartScreen fast-pass for code-signed apps in 2024, so reputation now builds from download volume, not from the certificate.)

A fix to the installer. The plug-in itself is unchanged from 0.2.0.

## The installer now asks where to put the plug-in

0.2.0 went Welcome, License, Ready, and never offered a destination: it installed to the standard VST3 folder and gave you no say. That is fine right up until your DAW is pointed somewhere else, and Ableton's **VST3 Plug-In Custom Folder** is exactly that case. An installer that cannot reach the folder your host actually scans is one that installs a plug-in you cannot load.

There is now a **Select VST3 folder** page, defaulting to `C:\Program Files\Common Files\VST3` with a Browse button beside it. If your DAW scans a custom VST3 folder, point Setup at that folder instead.

Upgrading from 0.2.0 is safe: Setup no longer reuses the previous install's remembered location, so it will not nest the new bundle inside the old one. If you installed 0.2.0 to the default folder, accept the default again and it lands in exactly the same place.

## Requirements

Windows, 64-bit. VST3 host (built and tested against Ableton Live). Rescan your plugin folder after installing.
