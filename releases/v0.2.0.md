> **Installing on Windows: about the SmartScreen prompt.**
> On first launch you may see a blue "Windows protected your PC" screen. This is normal for newer apps and does **not** mean anything is wrong. Keys is digitally signed by **OK Studio Inc.** Click **More info** and you will see that publisher name, then click **Run anyway** to install. The prompt stops appearing on its own as more people install. (Microsoft removed the SmartScreen fast-pass for code-signed apps in 2024, so reputation now builds from download volume, not from the certificate.)

---

Keys is a mouse-only playable MIDI keyboard. It makes no sound of its own: you click an on-screen piano and it emits MIDI to drive whatever instrument sits after it.

This is the first Keys build published anywhere. 0.1.0 was cut locally in July and never shipped, so everything below is what 0.2.0 is.

## Four arpeggiators, on one screen

Four lines, **A through D**, each in its own fixed colour (cyan, magenta, amber, lime) so a glance tells them apart across the bar, the macro cards and the step grid's playhead. Each has its own rate, shape, pattern, twelve launchable slots, chord and chain, so a four-way polyrhythm is one plugin instead of four.

- The **All** view puts all four on screen as a 2x2 grid of cards. The bottom row collapses to a 34 px strip when you need the height back, and folding the view never touches the lines: C and D keep playing.
- **Two harmony voices per line**, chosen from an interval list, each with its own chance knob.
- **Mutate** explores other notes of the held chord. Past halfway a stray can land a scale degree or two outside it, and past three quarters some strays turn chromatic, so the knob genuinely leaves the key at the top. **Lock** hardens whatever it found into the part, so a wrong-note lick you liked stops being a one-off.
- Hand a line a chord by **dragging** a chord card onto it. A line that is switched off still takes the chord in and starts the moment you switch it on.

## A step sequencer you draw

The **Draw** page is a twelve-lane per-step editor: note, octave, velocity, gate, ratchet, chance, transpose, lateness, harmony, chord, randomness and chain conditions.

- The **Note lane says notes**, writing `E3` rather than an index into a chord you cannot see.
- Its top half is **eight per-step shapes**, so a single lane can run up for four steps and down for four.
- Lanes have a **loop window, a direction and an on/off**, and a lane that is off keeps its drawing rather than being flattened.
- A **playhead**, per lane, honouring each lane's own length.
- **Copy, paste and reset** over a selected span; paste tiles, which is how a two-step figure fills eight.

## A chord library of 355 progressions

Browsable by **mood**, **genre** and what it does (loop, cadence, turnaround, vamp, lift, descent, turn, open). That third axis is the one that turns browsing into composing: "sad" is forty candidates, but "sad and it loops" and "sad and it ends" are two different requests.

Hear a whole progression by clicking its row, send it to the pads or to the generator's tray, star favourites, and ask **Follows** what could come after what you have already laid down.

## A chord generator with eight brains

Weighted pool, circle of fifths, Neo-Riemannian, progression templates, negative harmony, planing, Markov and the library. Note count, register and inversions apply to all of them, and voice leading runs over whatever any of them produced.

- A **sixteen-card audition tray**: try chords out without spending a pad.
- A **reference card** nothing regenerates, with **Similar** and **Could follow** beside it.
- Every filled card carries its **roman numeral**, so a row of cards reads as a progression.

## Everything else worth naming

- **Undo and Redo**, covering chord pads, arp lanes and arp slots: the things that destroy music.
- **REC**, because Ableton cannot record Keys onto the Keys track itself.
- **Copy and paste a chord** between pads, or save one out as a MIDI file.
- A **settings gear** with the plugin-level switches, an explicit update check, and the user guide.
- **Range knobs** on velocity, timing, strum and humanize: the band opens equally either side of the knob, so a line can sit a touch late and a touch louder with the variation landing on both sides.
- **Keys Host**, a standalone that runs a real instrument VST3 in-process, so a click makes sound with no DAW involved.

## Requirements

Windows, 64-bit. VST3 host (built and tested against Ableton Live). The installer places the VST3 where hosts scan; rescan your plugin folder afterwards.
